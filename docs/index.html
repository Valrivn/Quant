The Executive Narrative: The Seduction and Defeat of Market Timing

I started investing my personal savings into fundamental value stocks, but I quickly crashed into human psychology: emotions are not fully logical. Discretionary investing left me vulnerable to panic selling during steep drawdowns and greed during market rallies. I wondered: why leave capital allocation to ambiguous emotional impulses when Graham and Damodaran's principles can be formalized into code?

My first instinct was to use modern machine learning. I built models to predict price direction and adjust asset weights to time market dips. The backtest curves were astonishing—showing steep 80%+ win rates. But when I audited the data pipeline, I uncovered a humiliating truth: the backtest was a complete illusion.

The Look-Ahead Bias Trap: Standard Python financial libraries download auto-adjusted historical prices and restated 10-K financial statements. The model was accessing numbers that had been restated months after the trade date. Worse, it was implicitly peeking into future stock splits and cash dividends before they occurred. The machine was cheating by looking into the future.

Next, I attempted an "opportunistic" model: aggressively buying deep dips while using safety buffers (hysteria/hysteresis bands) to exit during crashes. This created a new failure mode: The Recovery Penalty. The stock market's highest-return days historically occur immediately after violent sell-offs. Because my model waited for volatility to cool before re-entering, it consistently missed the recovery bounce-backs. Any attempt to tweak thresholds to capture both was just curve-fitting historical noise.

That failure forced my ultimate breakthrough: abandon market timing completely. Market fluctuations are largely erratic. Real quantitative resilience comes from holding high-ROIC compounders with audited competitive moats, buffered by a 15% short-term Treasury cushion (BIL), and rebalancing quarterly on strict Point-in-Time SEC EDGAR data.

How the Current Production Strategy Actually Works (Debunking the Markov Myth)

Does the production engine use Markov chains or macroeconomic regime forecasters? No.

Early research (Phase 9 Sprint 6–8) heavily experimented with 2-state and 3-state Hidden Markov Models (HMM) trying to predict macroeconomic shifts from yield curves and credit spreads. However, empirical out-of-sample audits proved that Markov regime timing was fundamentally flawed:

  • Severe Whipsaw Lag: Statistical macro changepoints take 15–30 days of market pain to confirm, meaning Markov shields sold into cash after crashes had already occurred, and missed the initial sharp rebound days.
  • External API Fragility: An unhandled timeout from Federal Reserve FRED data caused the fallback model to misdiagnose 72 out of 102 months as a "BEAR REGIME," hoarding cash during bull markets.
  • Circular Validation: Early HMMs achieved a "fake" Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 1.0 because states were evaluated against labels derived from the same feature space.

The Problem: The legacy engine mixed intrinsic DCF cash flows with forum hype (w_hype = 0.475) in a single additive formula, conflating fundamental value with price momentum.

Jump-Diffusion Poisson Black Swans & TSMC Supply Chain Risk

The Problem: Traditional Gaussian models assume continuous bell curves, ignoring physical single-source choke points (e.g., NVIDIA's sole dependency on TSMC).
The Solution: Implemented a Jump-Diffusion Model pairing continuous Brownian motion with a discrete Poisson shock arrival rate (λ_stress = K_c × G_s), scaled by credit spreads. A catastrophe draw triggers an immediate -85% revenue wipeout in simulation, adjusting NVIDIA's probability of positive EVA from an unrealistic 100% to 77.35%.
Human Agency: AI trained on 5-year price history assigned 0% risk to TSMC because no outage had occurred. Human reasoning injected physical real-world vulnerability bounds.

August 24, 2026 • Decision 3 Source: DeepSeek Harness for Quant Pipelines

Deterministic Firewalls & Fail-Closed Discovery Gates

The Problem: Unconstrained LLM agent calls admitted speculative "whisper stocks" with zero SEC filings.
The Solution: Built a 3-gate fail-closed harness: (1) Organic Practitioner Test: mentions in technical subreddits (r/devops) vs retail hubs (r/wallstreetbets) ≥ 0.50; (2) Commit-to-Star Divergence: GitHub star spikes must have verified merged pull requests; (3) Solvency Brakes: minimum 1.5-year cash-burn runway.
Human Agency: LLM agents repeatedly hallucinated soft pass grades when financial data was missing. Human developers had to code explicit RuntimeError fail-closed exceptions.

September 27, 2026 • Decision 4 Source: HMM Validation and Regime Instability Fix

2-State Latent Gaussian HMM & Event-Driven Retraining

The Problem: Early HMMs reported a fake Out-of-Sample Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 1.0 due to circular validation against synthetic labels. Tested against true NBER recessions, ARI collapsed to ~0.0.
The Solution: Rebuilt into a 2-state Gaussian HMM trained on stationary rolling Z-scores (ΔBAA10Y velocity, 10Y-2Y slope, realized vol) clamped via tanh(z/2.0). Retraining was locked to scheduled FOMC/CPI release dates. Out-of-sample ARI recovered to 0.5175 with Crisis Recall of 98.5%.

September 28, 2026 • Decision 5 Source: Bond ETF Due Diligence Beyond Ratings

SEC EDGAR Look-Through Matrix for Corporate Bond ETFs

The Problem: Corporate bond ETFs (VCSH, VCIT) freeze during credit crunches, while agency credit ratings act as lagging indicators.
The Solution: Built an automated look-through scraper evaluating top 20 holdings to compute a portfolio-weighted Interest Coverage Ratio (Fund ICR). If credit spreads blow out (>300 bps) or ETFs fail ICR gates, capital automatically routes 100% into Short-Term U.S. Treasury Bills (BIL) or Physical Gold (GLDM).

October 11, 2026 • Decision 6 Source: Reverse-Engineering Alpha Through Survival Analysis

3D Compounding Survival Surface & Duration Hazard Models

The Problem: Binary "Win vs. Lose" metrics ignored holding duration—how long a company can sustain excess returns (ROIC > WACC) before competitive erosion sets in.
The Solution: Applied Cox Proportional Hazards and Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection (BOCPD) to model time-to-breakdown as a continuous geometric surface. The peak inflection ridge sets the exact mathematical boundary for the Competitive Advantage Period (N_CAP) in the downstream DCF engine.

October 12, 2026 • Decision 7 Source: Quantitative Backtest Performance Audit

Microstructure Slippage, Drift Deadbands, & Dynamic Regime Shield

The Problem: Flat 15 bps slippage understated execution drag during market stress, while daily rebalancing burned capital in fees.
The Solution: Computed dynamic square-root market impact slippage, enforced weight drift hysteresis deadbands (±15%), and automated a 3-stage de-risking shield: at -8% drawdown, equity caps at 50%; at -15% drawdown, equity caps at 15% (routing 85% to BIL Treasury Bills).

Why These Complex Decisions Were Pushed Out of the Final Production Release

A fair question in any engineering audit is: "If these 7 architectural designs were so mathematically sophisticated, why weren't they all pushed into the final production iteration?"

The answer is simple: the backtest results killed them.

In quantitative finance, theoretical beauty is worthless if it collapses out-of-sample. When we tested the earlier v7 DCF configuration, the backtest revealed catastrophic blind spots: sparse SEC queries loaded only 13–140 tickers per month instead of 1,000, unmapped companies defaulted to a hardcoded 8% WACC, and the strategy lost heavily ($-1.24\%$ to $-4.85\%$) with severe $-36.0\%$ drawdowns. The backtest itself was the scientific falsification tool that rejected v7.

Similarly, complex theoretical constructs—such as 3D Cox hazard manifolds, Poisson jump-diffusion simulations, and multi-state HMM Markov switchers—sounded impressive on paper, but introduced dozens of unobservable hyper-parameters that overfit historical noise and introduced single-point-of-failure API dependencies.

Occam's Razor triumphed over theoretical complexity: The backtest proved that a simple, deterministic system—85% high-ROIC value compounders, audited Lynch qualitative moats, a passive 15% BIL Treasury cushion, and an internal high-water mark drawdown circuit breaker—consistently outperformed hyper-complex academic models in real Point-in-Time conditions. We pushed out the unnecessary complexity and kept only what survived empirical reality.

The "Failure Lab": Honest Post-Mortems

Failures in the strategy logic and failures inside the backtest harnesses themselves.

Strategy Failure 01

Order Routing & Fee Churn

The Failure: Continuous monthly rebalancing on a $10,000 portfolio generated $14,704 in transaction fees—eating 43.8% of gross returns. The system was generating +26 bps in monthly gross alpha while paying 53 bps in execution friction!

The Fix: Opportunistic Swap Gate (Δz ≥ 0.60σ) and ±15% drift deadbands. Fees collapsed from $14,704 to $201 (0.44% of gains), preserving a +452% cumulative return.
Data Pipeline Defect 02

Look-Ahead Corporate Actions

The Failure: Convenient consumer APIs (yfinance) auto-adjust prices backward in time. When a stock split or cash dividend occurred in 2023, the API adjusted the 2019 price downward, allowing the algorithm to implicitly "know" future capital distributions.

The Fix: Scrapped consumer feeds. Ingested 83,000+ raw SEC EDGAR XBRL filings indexed strictly by public filing date timestamp (t_0), evaluating raw unadjusted transaction prices and booking cash dividends on verified historical ex-dates.
Harness Math Bug 03

Sortino Scaling Inversion

The Failure: Reporting scorecards showed an inverted Sortino ratio (0.42) that was half its Sharpe ratio (0.85). In parallel, an unhandled FRED API timeout caused the fallback to misdiagnose 72 out of 102 months as a "BEAR REGIME," hoarding cash in a bull market.

The Fix: Caught a bug dividing downside deviation by √12 twice in reporting (Sortino restored to 1.85); added disk caching for FRED BAA10Y credit spreads, cutting false bear months from 72 down to ~25.
