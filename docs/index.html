Value Investing Engine

A research project investigating whether fundamental Value Investing principles (Graham, Buffett, Damodaran) can be systematically automated in code—combining balance sheet fundamentals with qualitative business indicators to evaluate risk-adjusted performance against the S&P 500.

Project Overview • 30-Second Summary
1 • Research Objective

Automating Intrinsic Value

Tested whether algorithmic selection based on Point-in-Time cash-flow yields (ROIC − WACC), balance sheet durability, and business moat indicators can generate risk-adjusted returns without taking uncompensated beta.

Focus: Fundamental cash generation, not price timing.
2 • The Audit Discovery

Addressing Lookahead Bias

Early backtests showed an unexpected ~60% annual return. A code audit revealed that financial statement tables were accessing restated filings published after the simulated trade dates. The pipeline was rebuilt from scratch with strict point-in-time constraints.

Standard: Verifiable data integrity over cosmetic returns.
3 • The Verified Result

Capital Preservation Focus

Once sanitized, annualized return moderated to 11.57% (SPY: 13.00%). However, the engine reduced market sensitivity to a 0.63 Beta, limited maximum drawdown to −17.78% (vs. SPY −23.93%), and held down-market capture to 64.3%.

Outcome: Traded excess market exposure for downside protection.
Methodology & Verification • Why This Backtest Can Be Trusted

Backtests can produce distorted results when subtle temporal assumptions are overlooked. Below is an outline of the specific data controls, execution timing, and friction models enforced in this project:

System Architecture • Qualitative Indicators & Data Limitations
Step 1
Public Ingestion

Fetches SEC EDGAR filings via official API endpoints using the exact publication timestamp, alongside Treasury yields from FRED.

Step 2
Valuation & Risk

Calculates DCF intrinsic value, de-levers peer betas to isolate operating risk, and assesses qualitative business durability metrics.

Step 3
Fail-Closed Verification

Assets with missing indicators or unverified timestamps are excluded immediately. No synthetic fallbacks are permitted.

Step 4
Execution & Buffer

Constructs an equal-weighted basket of up to 25 undervalued companies, with a 15% Treasury buffer (BIL) and a 15% turnover deadband.

⚠️ Methodological Limitation • Qualitative Data Depth

A key constraint of this study is the historical depth asymmetry between financial and qualitative data.

While SEC EDGAR financial records provide standardized history back to 2010, alternative public APIs (such as patent feeds and consumer complaint logs) only provide survivor-bias-free data beginning around 2022. Retroactively imputing or backfilling qualitative scores prior to 2022 would introduce survivorship and lookahead bias.

Implementation Approach: To maintain strict data integrity, the engine partitions qualitative indicators to prospective periods where verified records exist, relying exclusively on Point-in-Time financial statements for the full 2018–2024 backtest.

Project Chronology • Methodological Challenges & Solutions
June 2026: Identifying Lookahead Leakage Issue: Restatement Lookahead Bias

Initial Observation: An early prototype produced an unrealistic ~60% annual return.

Diagnostic Finding: Financial statement tables were pulling restated values published months after the simulated trade dates, inadvertently trading with hindsight.

Remedy: Reconstructed the data ingestion pipeline to index strictly to SEC publication timestamps (filed_date).
July 2026: Evaluating Small-Cap Alpha Issue: Factor Beta vs. Genuine Alpha

Initial Observation: A screen targeting undervalued small-cap equities yielded 15.34% return, but incurred a −51.7% maximum drawdown.

Diagnostic Finding: Fama-French 5-Factor regression yielded an alpha intercept of +3.93% with t = 0.75 (p = 0.45), indicating that excess return was driven by unhedged market beta rather than selection skill.

Remedy: Removed discretionary small-cap tilt; introduced a 15% short-term Treasury allocation (BIL) to dampen portfolio drawdown.
August 2026: Managing Alternative Data Depth Issue: Survivorship & Historical Coverage Asymmetry

Initial Observation: Attempted to backtest unstructured sentiment and patent velocity across the complete 2018–2024 horizon.

Diagnostic Finding: Alternative datasets lacked standardized, survivor-bias-free coverage prior to 2022. Retroactively backfilling missing periods introduced data snooping.

Remedy: Quarantined alternative indicators to prospective periods; restricted long-horizon historical testing to audited SEC fundamentals.
September 2026: Resolving Data Loader Fallbacks Issue: Silent Default Values

Initial Observation: Multi-period walk-forward backtests unexpectedly collapsed to negative returns (−1.24%).

Diagnostic Finding: The data loader queried an incomplete universe partition. Missing fundamentals triggered silent defaults: cost of capital defaulted to WACC = 0.08, and missing classifications defaulted to "Technology".

Remedy: Constructed merged_pit_universe.parquet (1,000 tickers/month) and replaced silent fallback defaults with fail-closed exceptions.
October 2026: Purging Cardboard Dummies & Fallbacks Issue: Heuristic Placeholders in Validation

Initial Observation: An un-embargoed run reported an apparent 21.70% CAGR with high Fama-French alpha (t = 4.70).

Diagnostic Finding: An AST scan identified fallback placeholders: missing revisions returned 0.05, margins returned 0.60, and validation used a static oos_hit_rate = 0.62, granting unvetted assets portfolio entry.

Remedy: Permanently removed all placeholder defaults. Added dynamic square-root slippage and a 15% hysteresis turnover deadband. Returns settled at 11.57% with 0.63 Beta.
Mathematical Formulation • Core Equations & Plain-English Summary
1. Discounted Cash Flow & Reinvestment Growth (Damodaran)
$$\text{Value}_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\text{Cash Flow}_t}{(1 + \text{Discount Rate})^t} + \frac{\text{Terminal Value}}{(1 + \text{Discount Rate})^T}$$

Plain English: Calculates what future cash flows are worth in today's dollars, where growth must be funded by reinvested profits rather than assumed arbitrary growth percentages.

2. Bottom-Up Operating Beta (Damodaran)
$$\beta_{\text{business}} = \frac{\beta_{\text{stock}}}{1 + (1 - \text{tax})\frac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Equity}}}$$

Plain English: Strips out corporate debt to isolate the fundamental operating risk of the underlying business, preventing stock price noise from distorting cost of capital.

3. Market Impact Slippage Formulation
$$\text{Cost}_{\text{bps}} = \frac{\text{Spread}}{2} + \text{Fee} + \eta \cdot \sigma_{\text{daily}} \sqrt{\frac{\text{Trade Size}}{\text{ADV}}} \times 10{,}000$$

Plain English: Penalizes trading costs based on order size relative to average daily volume, ensuring realistic execution drag during volatile periods.

4. Market Sensitivity (β) & Downside Deviation (Sortino)
$$\beta = \frac{\text{Cov}(R_{\text{portfolio}}, R_{\text{market}})}{\text{Var}(R_{\text{market}})}, \quad \text{Sortino} = \frac{\text{Return} - R_f}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \min(0, R_{p,t} - R_f)^2}}$$

Plain English: Beta measures sensitivity to market swings (0.63 indicates 37% less drawdown exposure), while Sortino evaluates returns strictly against downside losses.

5. Cross-Sectional Permutation Null Hypothesis Test (Spiegelhalter)
$$p\text{-value} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \mathbb{I}\left( \text{Sharpe}_{\text{null}, b} \ge \text{Sharpe}_{\text{strategy}} \right)$$

Plain English: Randomly shuffles asset selections 10,000 times under identical market dates to test whether strategy performance reflects a valid signal or random chance.

Conceptual Lineage • Author Credits & Literature Foundations
Margin of Safety • Valuing Real Businesses
The Intelligent Investor
by Benjamin Graham (with Warren Buffett)

Provided the framework that equity shares represent fractional ownership of operating businesses. The concept of Margin of Safety establishes that purchases should only occur at a meaningful discount to intrinsic cash-generating capability.

Valuation Formulation • Cost of Capital
Investment Valuation
by Prof. Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern)

Supplied the quantitative mechanics for the valuation layer: capitalizing R&D over 3–5 year amortization schedules, de-levering industry sector betas, and linking growth directly to reinvested capital.

Business Cycles • 6 Corporate Lifecycles
One Up On Wall Street
by Peter Lynch

Informed the archetype classification engine: categorizing companies into 6 lifecycles (Fast Growers, Stalwarts, Slow Growers, Cyclicals, Turnarounds, Asset Plays) to avoid applying uniform valuation multiples across disparate business models.

Data Skepticism • Statistical Falsification
The Art of Statistics: How to Learn from Data
by Sir David Spiegelhalter

Established the testing framework for hypothesis validation: using permutation tests, p-values, and embargoed holdouts to evaluate whether empirical returns represent true inefficiencies rather than historical noise fitting.
