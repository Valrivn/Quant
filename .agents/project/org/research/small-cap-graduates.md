# Research Note: Small-Cap Graduates — What Takes a Small Cap to the S&P 500?

**Status:** DRAFT for CEO review
**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Prepared by:** hermes-bridge (synthesis of web research)
**Purpose:** Informing a discovery algorithm (alt-data + fundamentals) that finds small-cap companies on track to graduate to the S&P 500, built around **reinvestment rate × moat**.

---

## (a) Executive Summary

1. **Graduation is rare, but survivorship hides it.** Roughly 1/3 of small-cap index turnover over a decade is "graduation" to mid/large cap (Ranse 2026). Naive backtests on current constituents overstate returns by ~1-2%/yr for large caps and more for small caps; the survivor cohort earns dramatically more than the delisted cohort.
2. **The size premium IS the graduation effect.** The size premium is almost entirely generated by the small-caps that become large-caps; most small-caps simply don't. Finding them is the whole game.
3. **Reinvestment rate is the fundamental growth driver, but only when paired with ROIC.** Expected growth ≈ Reinvestment Rate × ROIC (Damodaran). Without quality-of-capital discipline, reinvestment becomes empire building — the asset-growth anomaly (Cooper, Gulen & Schill) shows high asset growth predicts *lower* future returns.
4. **Retained earnings predict returns.** Retained earnings-to-market subsumes book-to-market as a return predictor (Ball et al. 2020) — a direct, linkable reinvestment signal.
5. **Profitability matters over the 3-5yr horizon, not over weeks.** Unprofitable small caps can spike in speculative windows (2025), but profitable/reinvesting small caps compound best over 3-15 year windows.
6. **Horizon: 3-5 years is the right minimum.** Reinvestment→growth realization takes time; graduation itself takes years (Russell 2000→1000 via appreciation, then S&P 500).
7. **Do not buy the promotion event.** The S&P 500 index effect has reversed — additions now *underperform* post-inclusion. The alpha is in the pre-inclusion compounding, not the inclusion pop.
8. **Moat proxies are measurable.** ROIC > WACC spread, stable/expanding gross margins, and the five Morningstar moat sources are the qualitative overlay that separates compounders from zombies.

## (c) Common Traits of Graduates

Across Monster, Nvidia, Amazon, Netflix, Axon, O'Reilly, Chipotle, Intuitive Surgical:

1. **High reinvestment of cash flow** — Monster paid zero dividends (all cash into brand/distribution); Amazon/Nvidia/Netflix reinvested everything for a decade+.
2. **A durable moat forming early**: proprietary tech ecosystem (Nvidia CUDA), brand + distribution lock (Monster×Coke), content scale (Netflix), installed base + government contracts (Axon), specialty retail logistics (O'Reilly).
3. **High/improving ROIC above WACC** — the value-creating test (Damodaran; Morningstar).
4. **Distribution/partnership catalysts**: Coca-Cola→Monster (2015), PepsiCo→Celsius (2022). Category still growing + brand + distribution = S-curve.
5. **Capital discipline** — reinvestment funded from cash flow, not serial dilutive issuance (contrast with the asset-growth-anomaly losers).
6. **Long runway for reinvestment** — large TAM; category leader.
7. **Overlooked while small** — spent years under institutional radar before "graduating" (DPA Investments).

**Caution — the counter-evidence:** Morningstar doubts Celsius has a durable moat (brand intangibles); and *most* high-reinvestment small caps fail to graduate. Distribution partnerships raise the *likelihood* of success, not the certainty.

## (d) Reinvestment-Rate Evidence

- **Damodaran**: Expected growth (operating income) = Reinvestment Rate × ROIC. Reinvestment = (Net Capex + ΔNWC)/NOPAT. Growth needs both reinvestment *and* high quality of capital; "growth might require time to realize."
- **Equity version**: g = ROE × plowback ratio (retention ratio). Companies that retain earnings at high ROE compound; Buffett's 20%-ROE-80%-payout critique.
- **Retained earnings-to-market predicts the cross-section of returns** and *subsumes* book-to-market — significant for 5 years out (Ball et al. 2020, JFE). This is a direct reinvestment/retention signal.
- **THE WARNING — Asset Growth Anomaly** (Cooper, Gulen & Schill 2008): high *total asset growth* predicts *lower* future returns. Low-asset-growth quintile ~15.2%/yr vs high-growth ~7.1%/yr (spread ~8%/yr). Aggressive expansion funded by issuance/acquisition is a value *destroyer*. → Reinvestment must be qualified: **funded by operations, high ROIC, organic capex/R&D** rather than serial M&A + equity issuance.
- **See's Candies (Buffett, 1972)**: $25M purchase; by 2007 had thrown off $1.35B pre-tax with only $32M reinvested — proof that *low* capital intensity + *high* ROIC is the most valuable combination; reinvestment is most valuable when ROIC stays high at scale.

## (e) Horizon Evidence (3-5yr holding)

- **Reinvestment→growth realization lag**: growth from reinvestment "requires time to realize" (Damodaran) — supports multi-year holds, not quarters.
- **Profitability compounds over years**: over 6 years profitable + dividend-paying small caps compounded best while unprofitable firms were negative (WisdomTree, Jan 2026); high-quality quintile beat low-quality in Russell 2000 over 15 years through 2023 (Morningstar 2024).
- **Graduation takes years**: Nvidia ~11.5 yrs IPO→S&P; TSLA ~10 yrs, VEEV/WDAY ~12 yrs IPO→S&P 500 (Nasdaq); small-cap→large-cap via Russell 2000→1000 price-appreciation migration first, then S&P.
- **Small-cap cycles last ~9 years** on average; migration from Russell 2000→1000 re-ignites with interest rates >3% (Northern Trust/Daniel Fang, 2024-2025).
- **Conclusion**: a 3-5 year minimum holding period aligns with the reinvestment→compounding→graduation timeline. Trade signals on quarterly quality improvements, not monthly noise.

## (f) Promotion-Effect Evidence

- **The index effect has largely disappeared (or reversed):**
  - S&P DJI (2021), *What Happened to the Index Effect?*: median AD→ED excess returns fell from 8.32% (1995-1999) to **-0.04% (2011-2021)**. Post-ED excess returns were positive only for additions coming from S&P 400/600 (Group 1); negative for outside-S&P1500 additions.
  - TradingStudio (2026, 450 additions 2000-2025): additions *underperform* market at T+5 (-0.86%, t=-3.63) and T+21 (-1.43%).
  - NBER: "The Disappearing Index Effect" — inclusion effects declining over time; ETF/index-fund growth explains most of the decline.
  - Rollins DBA (Mecca 2024): effect disappeared over the last decade, tied to passive flows.
- **No permanent value effect** once pre-inclusion performance is controlled (NY Fed, Kasch & Sarkar 2012); price converges to value within ~2 months (McKinsey/Koller).
- **Implication for strategy**: Do NOT buy the promotion pop. The value is earned *before* inclusion (pre-inclusion EPS run +57%). A discovery portfolio should **hold through promotion** and let the S&P 500 + index funds passively bid it up — sell only on moat/quality deterioration, not on inclusion.

## (g) Moat / Qualitative Evidence

- **Morningstar Economic Moat** — five sources: (1) switching costs, (2) network effects, (3) intangible assets (brand, patents, licenses), (4) cost advantage, (5) efficient scale. Wide moat = 20+ yrs, narrow = 10+ yrs. Moat must show up as ROIC > WACC.
- **Buffett**: moat = sustainable competitive advantage; "time is the friend of the wonderful business"; See's example (brand + pricing power + low capital intensity).
- **Most firms create no excess value**: median ROIC ≈ 10% ≈ cost of capital (Morgan Stanley study). So ROIC>WACC is a *discriminating* screen, not a rubber stamp.
- **Moat + valuation**: Morningstar finds moat-rated undervalued stocks have a track record of outperformance vs. no-moat stocks.
- **Celsius counter-example**: Morningstar skeptical of durable moat (brand questioned) — shows the framework separates real compounders (Monster: narrow moat) from momentum fads (Celsius risk).
- **Operational proxies for the algorithm** (Equicurious): ROIC > WACC persistent spread; stable/expanding gross margin; pricing power; customer retention; category growth × market share; reinvestment funded organically. "Reinvesting <50% of NOPAT while ROIC > 15%" may indicate the moat is *too narrow to extend through growth* — i.e., high ROIC without a wide moat is a warning, not a buy.

## (h) Implications & Testable Hypotheses

### Implications for the discovery algorithm
1. **Base-rate discipline**: most small caps do NOT graduate. Every screening result is a candidate, not a certainty — portfolio-level diversification and survivorship-bias-free backtests are mandatory.
2. **Screen = Reinvestment Rate × ROIC × Moat proxy**:
   - Reinvestment: (Net Capex + ΔNWC + R&D)/NOPAT above peer median, funded from operations (quality: low net debt issuance, low M&A intensity).
   - Quality: ROIC > WACC; ROIC stable or rising over 3-5 yrs; retained-earnings-to-market high.
   - Moat: gross-margin stability, gross-margin-to-ROIC consistency, category growth, customer concentration (a positive — Boeing-style switching costs), distribution partnerships.
3. **Avoid** the asset-growth-anomaly profile (serial issuance + M&A + empire building) and pure unprofitable momentum (speculative windows decay over 3-5 yrs unless profitability inflects).
4. **Hold through promotion; never buy the pop.** S&P 500 inclusion itself is now a mild *negative* catalyst. Alpha is pre-inclusion compounding.

### Testable hypotheses (for the data-scientist)
- **H1**: Small-caps with Reinvestment Rate ≥ 50th percentile AND ROIC ≥ WACC AND (retained earnings/market cap) in top tercile outperform the Russell 2000 by >4%/yr over 3-5yr windows (net of survivorship).
- **H2**: Unprofitable small-caps with rising gross margins (profitability inflecting) outperform profitable-but-stagnant small-caps over 3-5yr windows.
- **H3**: Holdings sold on inclusion (buy-the-pop strategy) underperform holdings held through inclusion by >3%/yr over 12 months post-inclusion.
- **H4**: The reinvestment×ROIC signal subsumes naive asset-growth screens: controlling for reinvestment rate, high asset growth no longer predicts lower returns.
- **H5**: A 3-5yr fixed holding window dominates quarterly rebalancing on the same universe (net of turnover costs).
- **H6**: Moat-proxy overlay (stable gross margin + ROIC>WACC for 3 consecutive years) adds >2%/yr over the pure fundamentals screen.
